All three end up with the same floating strip for hidden icons. They differ in where you do the arranging.

You arrange right in the menu bar by ⌘-dragging, the same way macOS already works. A thin divider sits in the bar: everything to its left is hidden and shows up in the strip. You never open a window to do it.

A · Right in the menu bar

Nothing new to learn, and no settings window needed for layout. The catch: you're dragging tiny icons in a crowded bar, and the divider is always visible.

A small window shows your icons at a comfortable size in two rows. Drag between rows to hide or show, drag within a row to reorder. The real menu bar updates as you go.

B · A layout window

Thaw's approach, done cleanly. Big targets, easy to see everything at once. The cost is one more window to open.

Hold a modifier and right-click any icon to hide it (or show it, from the strip). Order still comes from ⌘-dragging in the bar. No divider, no layout window.
